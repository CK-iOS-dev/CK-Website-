Marcus Aurelius and Stoicism.

Firstly, you should know who Marcus Aurelius was, and then we will look at what he followed - Stoicism.

Marcus Aurelius : A Roman Emperor, who followed stoicism, he was the second Roman emperor to have done so. To put it into perspective, we can say that Marcus Aurelius ruled the known world to him, and it was the time of Pax Romannia. When Rome was a very dominant empire.

Marcus Aurelius, was simply the most powerful man of his time, yet he faced betrayals, suffering, and a war lasting 14 years, during his ruling period, which was about two decades. He was the last of the emperors known as “Five Good Emperors” to Rome.

Stoicism is a philosophy that normalises with suffering, involves self discipline, emotional restraint and the love of the fate. Let’s break it down ;

  1. Suffering - The unavoidable part of life.
  2. Self Discipline - Key pillar of stoic protocol.
  3. Emotional restraint - Knowing when to hold or release them.
  4. Love of fate - Embracing even the hardships of life.

It is also said, Stoics valued logic a lot. They said “To live a life worth living - a happy life, logic is important’. But they also used it selectively.

How did Marcus Aurelius practice that in his life?

Well, he was a man with absolute power over an empire, he reminded himself of his own death, for humility. He wrote personal journals, to keep himself disciplined. He saw challenges like an opportunity to act virtuously. While many preached theories, he practised stoicism as an emperor.
