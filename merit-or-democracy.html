Merit or Democracy.

Merit system is where merit gets the priority; whoever is actually capable gets to work on a big position. Leaders aren’t chosen just because they are popular; they are chosen because they are actually the ones who get the job done. Merit system looks at capable people, not popular people.

Democracy, on the other hand, treats popular leaders as the ones who are capable. It is not decided by merit who will be the leaders. It is decided by popularity whether someone is going to be the leader or not. This is the main problem with democracy.

However, we cannot say that democracy is only flawed, as it gives us the chance to change those who are sitting in power, which might be missing in a merit-based system. However, we can say that this is also a weak point in democracy as the power becomes ping-pong between two parties; they know that if they do bad things at this time, they will not come the next time, but the next time to the next time, they will definitely come, because there are only two options left for the people, so it is not like For the people by the people and to the people.

Yet merit system also has its flaws, such as if the wrong people are selected in the system, then the system will be ruined. However, merit works on the principle that whoever is capable will get selected, so I think that while it is a flow, we cannot say that a merit-based system is always likely to fail. In fact, merit system is not much likely to fail as only those who are really capable are selected. And there is another problem with this, and the problem is that merit of different people can be different, and is different for sure.

They can say that those who were born in backward families, how could they have equivalent merit compared to those who were born in affluent families? That concern is definitely valid, but I have to say that even in democracies, we see that those who were born in backward families do not possess much merit and aren’t given equal opportunity in democracy too.

Merit, and they do not also reach the top as well, so we have been lied to believe that anyone can reach the top, not anyone, not everyone can reach the top. Anyone can reach the top and that is dangerous as well, because anyone means anyone, and if the wrong people become popular and get selected, which we see happen in democracies very often, then it becomes a flawed system. There are a lot of examples.

What is best?

Well, the answer to that question is, neither is best, yet something is better. That if we create a form of government where merit is prioritised, over popularism with respect to democracy, then we can build a better model better than China, better than America.
