A Prince

A Prince is viewed differently from modern lens than it was from traditional sense. Modern lens says, Princes are hereditary. Princes don’t have much capability, they are there because of their family. They shouldn’t have right to rule by birth. But traditionally, it was believed that princes were chosen by the god to be the future kings, that is why they were born into king’s palace.

“A Prince” is about a story of a prince who had realised that there would no longer be his kingdom whom he dreams to be the king of, in the future. Because bigger states are waiting on the door to eat his kingdom, he would become a commoner. The argument those bigger states are proposing is that, his family doesn’t rule by the consent off the people, he cannot be removed, and that his family exploits the wealth of the whole principality/kingdom.

The prince admits those arguments are strong, at first glance, they appear very hard to be challenged. But then he argues that if his family rules the principality/kingdom without consent, it is still not legitimate reason for him to be removed necessarily. He argues, if consent is so important, then the bigger states should ask for a referendum, not capturing his principality/kingdom. The choice should still not be binary like “Stay with the current” and “Join the bigger states”. He argues there must be more than two options. The vote won’t be accepted on barely “I want this”. The vote will only be accepted if the reason is given, the vote would be like an exam. There must be a preparation time, so all can evaluate what actually makes sense.

He argues against the argument that he cannot be removed, he says “Certainly, not conveniently” but that doesn’t automatically make it a bad option, as it ensures long term stability. He argues that if we look at democratic structure, there seems to be a ping pong of power. Strong leaders are rare, and are often removed in about 10 years only. He argues that democracies often maintain an illusion of change in power but actually the structure remains the same and the ping pong goes on.

He states finally, consent of everyone or consent of the majority, don’t actually mean that the nation would be prosperous. It doesn’t guarantee economic growth. It doesn’t guarantee social order. He states that the democratic theory is one of the theories, not the best or the only one.
