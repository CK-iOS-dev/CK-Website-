Different levels of Freedom

On paper, all democracies have freedom. But the level of freedom isn’t similar, it is significantly different. Although, all democracies have set a limit on “free speech”, and it should be called limited speech, as it has limits, not freedom. But still, we cannot say that all democracies are bad, though I dislike democracy, it has a ping pong of power, power often shifts from one idiot to another. Democracy is where a half paralysed system, it cannot be replaced, but cannot be fixed either, because its structure might not allow, it often hides behind “nationalist” “your own” “rule of people” and often outlasts a system especially a meritocratic one that can eventually be removed, and is removed entirely. Yet I would like to appreciate those democracies that have, at least, tried to fulfil their promises, at best, possible.

Britain’s promise : It states there is freedom of speech, though there is a limit. I would address it as limited speech, this limited speech allows people to have some freedom, that is unusual in its own way. There is monarchy, but songs like “God save the queen” by Sex pistols, can be played, but abolition of monarchy can be argued, even protests can be organised. There are 4 countries in the UK, but one country - Scotland, wanted to be independent, as there was a mass protest, though the referendum resulted in favour of staying tin the UK, yet Scottish National Party remains, and often wins significant seats in Scotland. Prime Ministers are mocked on daily basis, but I haven’t yet heard who was punished for that. People from former colonies, come and argue against the empire Britain had, yet it is very normal and such issues allowed in even major institutions like Oxford, Cambridge, and even the Parliament. England has a state church, but many in England are atheists openly, and are very much common. The UK is very diverse of a nation, in fact more diverse than many who claim diversity by uniting those who could be different countries themselves. In Britain, it is not believed generally that the UK is somewhat a natural or “had to be there” thing, almost everyone knows the nation is a pure construction, that happened due to a marriage between two royal families. People can protest, even in front of Parliament, because there is a specific place for that, and unless the boundary is not crossed, nobody is harmed. PMs are often held accountable, they face significant scrutiny, the media isn’t sold out, various independent sources, and even the BBC which is publicly funded, continue to raise people’s voice. There is less of a blasphemy hurdle for atheists. Secularism is de facto, not de jure.

Now compare that to South Asian or African countries, in most of those nations, one cannot argue in favour of monarchy and argue the abolition of republics who they are. Provinces cannot call a referendum if the demands of breaking apart are raised, and are often suppressed, not even counterparts like Scottish National Party can exist in most of those nations, you can generally not mock the president in these nations, the way you do in the UK, you cannot argue in favour of the British Empire’s good things or if you believe that the empire was okay overall, you cannot argue that in institutions like universities, let parliament alone. In most of these nations, PMs aren’t held as accountable as in the UK, and mockery is a big hurdle, and if you are atheists or something, you must acknowledge blasphemy, and in most of these nations, atheism is sidelined as “irreligion” despite them calling themselves “secular”, these on paper secular states run heavily on religious and identity politics. It is believed in those nations that they “had to be there”, and isn’t acknowledged that they are a construction and came into existence due to an actor they call “external”.

So, the difference is clear, and is substantial.
