Aristotle, Plato, Socrates : Actually Wise or Overrated?

I begin with Aristotle, then Plato and finally Socrates. Before I will point out the core ideas and stress test them simultaneously. Read it full.

Aristotle :

Core Ideas ;

1. Study the real world, not abstract perfection :  He believed that knowledge comes from observation and classification. This is actually a great idea, living in myths gives no knowledge even if it might look like fun or entertainment. Observing the reality is actually a thing one should do. No problem here. Yet many people think that their own experience is the right one, and that one bad experience becomes a lifetime generalisation.

2. Everything has a purpose : Well, it was always thought that humans have a purpose, earth has a purpose and when they figured out there is something beyond earth, they again said there’s a purpose. But they never question what is the purpose of the last purpose. This idea could become problematic, as people won’t question what they consider is provides that purpose. Purpose is subjective. Not absolute or universal.

3. Virtue lies in Golden mean : Excellence is balance between extremes. That contains truth to some extent yet if I apply it to itself, then it appears problematic. Here is how ; a person would think that they need to balance things, they might not actually follow the principles. They would cherry prick and frame it like they are balancing between extremes. As I said, if I apply balancing to itself, there is something we can say, isn’t always avoiding extremes an extreme itself? A snake that bites itself.

4. Humans are rational and social animals : All animals are social, even the “lone wolf” term is wrong, even most wolves have a group. Lion, whom people consider the one who is enough alone, also has a family and group. Even lionssometimes hunt in groups. Being social is unique by no standards. Regarding humans being rational, only a bit. Humans often excuse their mistakes by saying “it is human nature”. This is not rational. Humans apply rationality, selectively. Where it takes away their comfort, they kick out rationalism. A dog can say “it is dog nature” if he’s done something inappropriate, because he can’t think about what he’s done. A human can. Those are different. And yeah, every animal has some rationality that helps them survive, humans are no different. No, humans are not rational, and being social isn’t unique.

5. Happiness (eudaemonia) :  Aristotle believed that happiness is the highest goal. Well, we can believe that we all want happiness in our life, but that could be interpreted by hedonists like we should want comfort all the time, and we should not work hard, because the end goal is happiness, and we can get it just straight away in this modern world, where there are a lot of things that can comfort us very easily. So this might get misinterpreted by many people, and I should say that whether happiness is the highest goal or not matters person to person, it is not true that happiness is the only highest goal.

6. His Philosophical centre :  He believed that human flourishing comes through rational moderation and practical engagement with reality. Good, but what we have seen is not quite moderation, but extreme of moderation. How practical!!

Core Ideas ;

Plato :

1. Reality has two levels :  Plato believed that there are two levels of reality, imperfect physical world, perfect world of poems and ideas. Well, how could there be two realities, we can see that the physical world will live in, is the actually the one who is real, otherwise perfect world does not exist, and forms and ideas are our imaginations, not reality necessarily. So I refute the claim that there are two levels of reality. I see only one level of reality, and that is the material world we live in, not perfect world of ideas, poems, and ideals, because that is our own imagination, not the physical reality.

2. The ideal is more real than the material : Plato believed ideals are more real than material things, but I argue that ideals are not always practical and must evolve with society. Physical things, though they decay, are tangible and real. Otherwise, the abstract truths could be created anytime, and once an idea has been created, how can we destroy an idea? How can we destroy what does not exist? Physical things decay because they exist, abstract truths cannot decay because they don’t even exist.

3. Philosopher Kings : Plato’s idea of philosopher kings ruling based on wisdom is flawed, as wisdom is subjective and power corrupts. While majority rule is imperfect, it is preferable to a merit-based system where a few hold power, as democracy, despite its flaws, is the best available option.

4. Justice is harmony : Plato believed that society works when every class performs its proper role. However, we all know that Plato did have slaves, and what does he mean by every class performs its proper role? It means that the Greek Empire, he lived in, was very much classified into different classes, rich class, poor class, slave class. And I do not think that Plato himself had that justice which provides harmony no, he didn’t. It was he had slaves. Now the readers would argue that it was a norm at that time, so what? Philosophers are there to question the norms, not to follow them blindly.

5. Reason should dominate emotion and desire : Plato believed that reason should dominate emotion and desire, rational order creates both personal and political stability. Well, I disagree with this idea, because rational order will not create personal or political stability necessarily, because when we keep questioning things around us, It instead puts us in more trouble than making our life easier, often that happens that the people start questioning the political order, and they find out that what they were told that the system is based on fairness and equality. They do not find it true. And the result comes up like they want anarchy, they become nihilists so I do not think that reason should dominate everything. I think that it is itself a desire to be rational, and it is itself a desire to end desire, even if you call it reason. Emotions should also not be restrained and be called rational if they are controlled because restraining one’s emotions makes one’s life worse off.

6. His philosophical centre : Plato believed that human life should be organised according to eternal rational ideals.Yet I believe that Plato did not challenge all the norms that were being forced in that era of society, didn’t question why there are slaves. He didn’t question himself. So what rationality is he talking about? Is it the rationality of a kind that is applied selectively? Yeah, that is very common in humans.

Socrates :

Core Ideas ;

1. ”Know thyself” :  Socrates believed that self examination is the foundation of a meaningful life. As mentioned earlier, that philosophers like Aristotle and Plato did not question themselves, but here we see something new, that Socrates is saying that one should question oneself to make a foundation of a meaningful life. Yet this is not without any flaws.It can become a paralysis situation where one keeps examinating oneself for all his life. And what comes in the end is nothing, because endless examination creates endless doubt, leading to criticising oneself very often, and not landing onto a decision.

2. Wisdom begins with recognising ignorance :  He believed most people falsely think they know things yet he didn’t think that the same logic could be applied to himself. He famously said that he who knows that he knows nothing is a wise person. Well, he who knows nothing. How is he a wise person? How could Socrates be a wise man then? what if we apply the same logic to him? His logic doesn’t hold.

3. Truth emerges through questioning (dialectics) :  Socrates said, constant dialogue exposes contradictions. Well, if you look at Socrates’ own principles, then we find out that if he himself had continued the questioning, the dialogue, then so many contradictions would have appeared every single time, so what is the truth? And it itself is a contradiction.

4. Virtue is equal to knowledge :  People do wrong because they are ignorant, not because they consciously choose evil, socrates believe that. Yet I say that many virtuous people and nations have been hippocratic and evil.They have chosen the virtue selectively, and what virtue is differs from person to person, there is no virtue that is universal to all. Several people in history have used the word virtue to fulfil their own endeavours. what has been the virtue has been unknown so I say there is no virtue. There are subjective interpretations of virtue.

5. The unexamined life is not worth living : He believed that reflection is essential to human being. Now, this is what really contains truth to some extent that reflection is necessary in the life of a human being otherwise, what is the difference between a human being and a dog? I just mentioned an example earlier within the discussion about Aristotle, and I said that if a human being excuses his flaws by his nature, tendency, then what is the difference between a dog and him because a dog cannot question whether he has done the right job or not, but a human can take a look at what he’s done, but often. many people do not.

6. His philosophical centre : Socrates believed that rational self inquiry as the path to moral truth, well, this contains truth to some extent, however, have we ever seen someone like Aristotle, Plato, Socrates, question themselves. No, we do not find such a historical incident where they question themselves.

If you have read this far : Conclusion becomes that Aristotle, Plato, and Socrates were not wise but considered them wise. Whether you do, matters upon you. Yet I must say they were not the perfect beings that should be our authority.
