Happy Birthday!! Or another ruined day?

It has become a tradition in the last few centuries, it has become very normal to celebrate birthdays, it is the day when you celebrate the exact date you were born on. At first glance, it seems like almost all birthdays are happy. But I think that assumption is inherently wrong. Most birthdays are not happy, not all birthdays are happy, and not everyone’s is.

What do I mean here?

I simply mean, sometimes it can be true when the celebration is entirely external, when the celebration is not for oneself, when the party is not for you. It is not always realised but it is very common of an experience. You believe it is for you, but it is actually to serve a different purpose. Which purpose? The purpose chosen by others, not you.

But one asks then : There have to be people around, otherwise is it even a celebration?

That concern is valid, I don’t think it is irrational either. How about one’s own agency? One’s own wish to organise annual event like, one’s own intuition about how it should be. We aren’t assuming that a person would assume so many ambitious things in a celebration like birthday, but control is something one should have. Control, over how it must be organised, what can happen and what cannot. A person has to be wise enough to raise this concern.

Many would read this article, and think “Well that is what usually happens” but that is an explanation not a justification. Explanation tells why it happened. Justification is evaluating whether what happened was just or it wasn’t.

The questions to ask :

Did things go the way I wanted them to be?

Did I enjoy it?

If possible, would I want to do it again?

What reason do I have beyond “it is normal”?

Because I have experienced such birthdays myself where things were external, not in my hand, though they could be. From now on, I understand this subject, I wrote this to connect with those who have experienced similar. If you never felt like that, you can still learn from it.

This article is strongly not personal, it is almost universal. I mean, common.
