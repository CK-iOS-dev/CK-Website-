Promise vs Delivery

The promise was freedom, the promise was consent, the promise was legitimacy, the promise was the rule of people. The delivery doesn’t match it, it is hypocrisy how significant promises are made as if they could certainly be achieved, when you ask that why haven’t those promises been fulfilled? The reply is that human systems are imperfect, but expecting perfect implementation is too absolute. Then why have we been promised a lot more than we can be practically delivered.

For example,

I don’t have much problem if a government says “No free speech”, your speech must be restricted because we prioritise social order in our very diverse country, though every country is diverse today, and significantly so. I would accept “restricted speech” because the state has been honest about that. If explicit acceptance is impossible, they must not, at least, have a rule saying “Freedom of speech”.

I don’t have much problem if a government says “We must not expect too high economic growth and inflation could be higher, we must try to fix it, but we aren’t sure” because the state has been honest, this is not irrational at all. At least, it is better than saying “The inflation will be 3% while growth would be 6%” and later not delivering it, or at least if they say so, they must say that it is not guaranteed.

I don’t have much problem if the state says “You see, we are the government, you don’t run us. You can only elect those who run you, you can even change them. You can change individuals and parties, but not the government”. This is honesty and practically yes, it is so. If not explicitly, then, at least, it shouldn’t be proposed that people are the rulers, because they aren’t.

I don’t have much problem if the state says that We lied, we spoke about ideals that were theoretical, practically the things are way different, but what can be done? This is how governments run. Now that the state has been formed, we tell you cannot avoid this structure even if you remove ours, somehow, in some form, it would persist. Instead of being idealistic, we must be a bit real. It is honest. If not explicitly, the state should not talk about ideals anyway.

I don’t have a problem with how a government practically works, I don’t have problem with power, I don’t have much problem with imperfection. I just hate sweet words, but execution like that. Don’t talk about ideals, if you end up using the very structure you promised to change but you cannot.
