Minimal Clutter

You see, we are proposing this concept through an example, Steve Jobs. You’d better understand it this way.

Steve Jobs (Steven Paul Jobs) was a person who got obsessed with Zen Buddhism in his teenage years, it was the Hippie era. He went to India for his spiritual journey. He was back a year after. He had then decided to “Think Different” and live on his terms, as he said that he had nothing to lose at the time, he started Apple. He was not so far from being rich, and just at the age of 23, he sat on a million dollars in the later 70s.

Steve still didn’t change his lifestyle. He slept on the floor, it took him 8 years to decide a sofa, focused on one task, and very deeply so. He didn’t want to do everything, he didn’t want to change everything. He wanted to do something specific with all his effort, discipline, and focus.

He wanted to make things intuitive and powerful at the same time, he wanted to make them direct but still beautiful. Just like magic, and that is how a person who had dropped out of Reed college, eventually starts something, and turns giant computers into desktops, then laptops and finally ultra portable devices like iPads and iPhones.

What I am not saying?

Zen Buddhism is good.

India is actually spiritual.

Vegan diet makes sense.

You’ll create the world’s largest corporation.

I just mean, clutter should be minimal. What you don’t need, don’t sacrifice for it.
