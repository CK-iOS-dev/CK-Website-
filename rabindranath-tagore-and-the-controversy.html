Rabindranath Tagore, and the controversy.

Rabindranath Tagore, perhaps the first Indian to get a Nobel price in literature. He was a philosopher, poet, song writer, author. He was a polymath, as many consider him. He was very influential man, his written song “Jana Gana Mana” is the national anthem of India. But that doesn’t make this man a clean and saintly.

The controversy : The song, was sung in front of the Emperor of India at that time. In 1911, Delhi Durbar was set, where he sang the song. It was the time when King George V (Emperor of India 1910-36) had changed the capital city of India from Calcutta to Delhi. The Durbar of 1911, was attended by almost every Indian king, British - Indian officials and famous figures like Rabindranath Tagore himself. He sang the song, and later the news were published that he praised the emperor of India through his song lyric “Bharat Bhagyo Bhidhata” meaning the dispenser of destiny of India. And looking at the political context of the time, it seemed who it was, despite not being mentioned explicitly.

Tagore was honoured by Knighthood and Nobel prize. Though he gave up Knighthood after the Jallianwala Bagh incident of 1919. And later gave clarifications on his song lyric “Bharat Bhagyo Bhidhata” stating that it didn’t mean a British monarch, but either the people of India or the divine, he stated it in 1937, King George V was dead then.

My objection is :

Anyone can play this strategy, first get rewarded and later deny. Because the words were not explicit, neither could be proved. And you see, if people stay in the heart of people, it doesn’t make any sense. If he meant divine, then no divine is there, shared by all.

The national anthem of India could be more neutral and secular, especially in the terms of language. No referendum was ever held, whether the nation wants top adopt the song of a specific writer. He does mention various parts of India though, but that is not enough. This was not established through consent.

I, however, don’t say that “Jana Gana Mana” is not the national anthem of India, as it officially is. But I absolutely have the right to criticise the flaws. I absolutely can have a different view. I surely can argue anything, as long as I don’t incite any harm.
