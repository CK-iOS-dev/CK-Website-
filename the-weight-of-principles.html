The weight of Principles

Some of us reading this, have already thought about principles and whether we should go on to live a life by principles, but likely a few questions rise when we think of applying it in the real world. The question is often, what is the cost of it?

I say, I’ll explain it withe examples but here’s a quote :

“Principles are not easy to live by” (C.ee K.ay)

Now examples, let’s choose a particular principle - Honesty.

Few questions rise again :

1. Are we expecting an absolute practice?

2. Should we be honest for a significant portion of our life or in significant events?

3. What is our definition?

4. What definition is correct?

My answers are that,

1. Absolute practice is very very hard. Yet I cannot say it doesn’t exist. I’d rather advise not optimising for it. But still we can consciously choose to be honest or not, in most situations, yet it is often not a beneficial path.

2. This question matters, let me answer, if a person has acted mostly honest, but when he saw that his lying or hypocrisy could have big positive impact on his/her life and their loved ones, should they do it? if you think, that significant portion matters. If e assume that portion to be majority of their acts. At the same time, a human being who has not done a crime for most of his life, and has done it now due to some reason, is still a criminal. I acknowledge both are important.

3. Our definition is what we follow usually. We don’t strictly follow the dictionary. For example, to somebody it could be still honesty even if they admit their hypocrisy after years. For others, it could be discouraging.

4. If we stick to our definition, the problem is that we might create a flexible one, and the one which might align with our biases. But still fit the category of that particular principle somehow. If we stick to dictionary’s definition, then the problem is that we don’t know who build it and who didn’t. And why is that correct? And our is not.

I must state it clearly, living a principled life is very hard. And often it is not the prudential choice. You pay expensive price for it, but if you value consistency, coherent frameworks, and logical value then you will prefer to live a life as described by “[C.ee](http://c.ee/) K.ay”.

The questions and the answers are very much relevant to almost any principle there is. Honesty was a mere example. Otherwise, it would be too boring and quite difficult to understand.

Do you think that such a life is worth?
