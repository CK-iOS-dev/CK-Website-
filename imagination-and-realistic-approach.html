Imagination and realistic approach.

Imagination in this context refers to ideas we think about, and we try to apply them in reality but we struggle to do so, because the idea was not realistic. It was aspirational. It was utopia, impractical in reality. Realistic approach here means targets are not too ambitious, they are a little hard to achieve but quite achievable. They are not optimistic assumptions, nor they are pessimistic assumptions.

If someone argues there is equality, how do we figure out whether it is imaginative or realistic? There are two ways people answer this question - 1. People hold the view that equality exists it is also held by most philosophers and ethicists, 2. Evidence and Reason. If we say “most” people believe in it and it is true, that is a wrong approach, it is a majoritarian one, not factual or truthful.

Evidence and Reason - This is what can be trusted. Evidence supports that humans are not biologically equal, some have better genes than others. Socially, it seems that they are equal, but a father remains father, son remains son always. Politically, an MP doesn’t have as much power as a Prime Minister. Legally, a lawyer and a judge aren’t equal. What equality?

This is imaginative and aspirational.

Realistically, humans are not equal. But it serves as a great tool when one says “All are equal” “Humanity” is one. A person might pay for the equality, another might get something for it. For example, a person in the UK earns £100,000 annually, he pays £40,000 in taxes. Another person earns £50,000 annually, and pays £10,000 taxes. This is due to the tax laws. Will the one who pays £40,000 in taxes get 4 times better treatment? Not realistically.

You can give many examples, but I want to say that imagination and reality are different, we often forget this.
