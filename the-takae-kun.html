Conceptual Essay

The Takae Kun

A fictional doctrine on authority, equality, and the household.
30 August 2026   ·   Conceptual

Takae Kun, a boy who grew up watching the rise of feminism, women's rights, and was taught that human beings are equal, regardless of who they are, male or female. But Takae Kun was a male; he couldn't have accepted something that would have weakened his position. Over time, he develops a new system which was highly based on traditional patriarchy. It was similar, but not the same.

Before proposing it, Takae Kun thinks about something:

Three Possible Worlds

The system or world could have the following possibilities of dynamic:

  1. The world would be male dominated, meaning the number of men in power or most fields would exceed that of women.
  2. The world would be dominated by women, in the same way.
  3. The world would have equal or near-equal numbers and strength of both genders.

That's it.

Now he comes to the development of his own system. But let's get a flashback of who Takae Kun is.

Takae Kun is a 45-year-old man who has built his own house and holds pride in owning it. He often describes the house payment years (15 years) as a war. He's won it. But Takae Kun is single.

The Takae Kun Doctrine

He is a proud man. He disregards equality concepts.

"Treat fairly, not equally."

He gives a Cold War example. In the Soviet Union, the commoners were equal. But what was the standard of their lives? And in the US, the commoners were not equal, yet they had a better standard of living, though it differed significantly.

But then he talks about Britain. He describes how that nation calculates on defence, has nukes, is a permanent member of the UN, is embedded in alliances, and has substantial historical influence. In that nation-state, the standard of living was also maintained; healthcare and education were ensured for free.

He uses this example to describe:

  1. A nation-state with equality, but not high living standards.
  2. A nation-state with high living standards for even commoners, but not equality.
  3. A nation-state with high living standards for commoners, though relatively less inequality than the second.

He describes that his household cannot provide equality, because he worked hard to build his house. And whoever lives in there cannot be equal to the person who built it. They haven't yet worked hard. He makes an exception for lodgers, because they pay for living.

He also describes that his household cannot be the United States either, where immense inequality can persist. However, he doesn't discourage it completely. He rather chooses the Great Britain model.

The Household Arrangement

Takae Kun is the sole owner of the house. His spouse, who is necessarily a female, won't have any ownership right over the house. Takae would not cover her expenses. He would not take the obligation to defend her physically necessarily. She cannot share a room with the Kun unless she has special permission. Kun won't ask her to.

She cannot cook meals for the Kun unless permitted. She cannot shout at the Kun, let alone use physical violence. She can appeal and petition, but not "order" or "force". The Kun has the right to carry a black thick stick, as a tradition he himself started.

The Kun won't give his salary to her. The Kun would give a little amount occasionally if he wishes.

Takae Kun, the sole owner, would not be called by name. Not "Takae", not "Takae". He would have a special title: the Kun. No nicknames or name allowed unless permitted.

This sounds way too much by modern standards. But it doesn't mean his female partner is suppressed. She has basic rights which cannot be denied:

Basic Rights

  • She must have a personal room, and guaranteed basic things like food and clothing. The Kun secretly retains a key for that room, for emergencies.
  • She has the right to make money, and Takae demands not even a penny as charity. However, a loan won't be denied if needed.
  • She cannot be shouted at, as that violates the peace of the household. But if it is broken already by her, then the case is different. That applies as an emergency law.
  • She won't have a common name. She would be called "the Miss" or another title, if approved by Takae.
  • She can give suggestions without fear, but those suggestions would not be final.
"By agreeing to these terms, she gets free house, guaranteed basics (if she doesn't earn), right to earn, her own space. That's it. If those aren't enough, I don't care. They remain how they are."

Takae's View on Human Equality

Takae's view on human equality: It would be insufficient to consider an entire race, sex, or nation as either equal or superior to the other. And that is subtle; it has to be judged case by case.

He says:

"Men = Women, is normative truth"

Not the one which is defensible regardless of the norm. He doesn't want women to have rights. He wants one type of rights: human rights.

He strongly believes that if women slapped a man, that's generally not viewed like domestic violence. But the opposite is generally perceived like that. It shouldn't happen.

End of essay.
