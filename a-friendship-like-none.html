A Friendship like none

There were two friends who met in elementary school in Japan, they shared on idol - Osamu Tezuka, one obsession, and one dream. For decades they remained one era, though they were two, signed under one pen name for decades, created something massive like Doraemon, and Ninja Hattori like anime. They kept writing mangas all their life, made the manga industry so popular that the world today knows what mangas are, and people do recognise Doraemon.

Hiroshi Fujimoto, and Abiko Motoo were those two friends who were behind all these mangas, and those shared interests. What’s interesting about there friendship, is not how it started. But how it lasted, all their life. They worked for their projects in one room, shifted to Tokyo together, lived in the room together, and even shared clothes sometime. They were soon introduced by their “master” to the manga publishers, and developed their initial mangas under the name “Ashizuka Fujio” which was inspired by their master’s pen name “Tezuka (Hand - Te, Zuka - Suffix)” which meant feet (Ashi) of the master. They remained lifelong admirers of Osamu Tezuka, and later they changed their pen name into Fujiko Fujio, and as they shared almost everything, they shared 50/50 of the profits. Nobody knew who was gonna be more successful, yet they made the promise. Hiroshi Fujimoto’s Doraemon became a huge success, while Abiko Motoo’s Ninja Hattori became popular as well. But Doraemon became a massive one. Still profits were shared equally. Both Hiroshi Fujimoto and Abiko Motoo became immensely popular and wealthy, under a shared pen name “Fujiko Fujio”. The rule was simple, the profits will be shared equally regardless of who wrote what. Until 1986. In 1986, Hiroshi Fujimoto was diagnosed with liver cancer and heart issues. They eventually decided to split, and settle the copyright matters before one of them dies. They made different pen names, and did independent projects, Hiroshi Fujimoto focused mostly on Doraemon. While Abiko focused on Ninja Hattori only till 1987. While they had splatted, they did respect each other’s creative work. Abiko Motoo once said “I don’t read Doraemon, cause my work can get inspired by it”. Despite different craft philosophies, they continued to regard the creativity. In 1996, Hiroshi Fujimoto was working on a new Doraemon film, but he collapsed on his desk, and was pronounced dead by the hospital. Abiko Motoo was asked to continue his friend’s work, but he said that only Fujimoto himself could make it like that. And he’d like to focus on his own work. Abiko remained active till many years, but passed away in 2022. Both friends are believed to have died painlessly.

The unusualness :

Doraemon was a huge hit, still profits didn’t go to Hiroshi Fujimoto only, they were split 50/50. Though Abiko Motoo also shared the profit of his project 50/50.

When Fujimoto’s health declined, Abiko agreed to not continue 50/50 further, as he had enough money already, but he could have denied, he didn’t.

The friends remained friends lifelong, and despite the differences in philosophy of craft, they regarded each other. The bond didn’t end.

Not all splits are bad or unfair.
