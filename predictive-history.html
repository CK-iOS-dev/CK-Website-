Predictive History — Jiang Xueqin

On historical patterns, political analysis, intellectual frameworks, and the limits of prediction.
13 November 2026   ·   Commentary

Let me describe why I think he is a flawed person.

He thinks that things which have happened in the past are likely to happen again in similar ways. For example, he believes that a global collapse is coming. He often gives simplified views, missing important nuances.

He believes that the billionaires around us are not actually genuine independent actors, but agents through which the government transferred technology. He uses the internet as an example. His explanation is that people would dislike the US government if it directly supplied the internet while opposing the Soviet Union.

Video reference: Watch the video on YouTube

Basically, I mostly believed that Jiang was honest about his speculation, while still finding some of his views incomplete and less convincing. But now the tables have turned.

Of course, there must be a boundary one must not cross. I found something absurd about him, which exposed how he often misses nuance and makes simplified conclusions.

The Ranking of World Leaders

In a recent video with Sneako, he ranked world leaders in tiers. The tiers ranged from S to D.

In S tier, he placed leaders such as Mao, Hitler, Stalin, Khomeini, Trump, Netanyahu, Ashoka, and Genghis Khan. His explanation was that they inherited something broken and transformed their nations into powerful ones.

But that is not the whole story. He openly ignored the negative impacts and did not emphasise them. He focused almost entirely on the maintenance of power, stability, and the transformation of nations, while ignoring the human cost.

In the box of leaders he was choosing from, there was no Churchill. How? Ranking people like Ashoka or Genghis Khan as S tier, yet Churchill was not even among the options.

He ranked Gandhi as S tier, but Gandhi held no formal position in the country. One should not confuse political influence with political power.

There were leaders like FDR and LKY, but he did not rank them. What is that? Seriously?

Video reference: Watch the world leaders ranking video

God, the Afterlife, and Love

Then I saw a podcast video with the thumbnail: You Have 5 Years to Live. It looked like Jiang had said it. However, I did not conform to that idea.

I looked forward to hearing his views about God, the afterlife, and love. I already knew that Jiang liked Dante. But he had never told us whether he believed in such things or not. I had suspected this for a long time, yet I found him to be an honest guy with fewer problems.

But I confirmed my doubts in that video. His explanation seemed very basic and, in my view, flawed.

"You cannot convince me that there is no God. How can there be beauty, love, kindness? I do believe that there is a God."

Regarding the afterlife, he says:

"When a person dies, they get a new beginning. The reason it is hidden is that, if one knows that death leads to a new beginning, one won't care whether they are poor or whatever."

I do not think that anyone serious would be convinced by these explanations alone.

Money as an Abstraction

He once said, "Money is an abstraction." They can print infinite money. They do not do it because otherwise you cannot point towards some people and say, "Oh, work hard, study hard, or you will end up like this."

That is not quite on point. Money without enough resources is abstract. If we print a quadrillion dollars, our problems would not disappear in terms of money. What is the value of money if there is no restriction?

Video reference: Watch the video on money

The Anglosphere, Japan, Germany, and India

He often does not speak favourably about Anglosphere countries, and describes their people as "egoistic". "Oh, I'm Anglo-Saxon."

He thinks that Japan and Germany will rise again, despite their population constraints. He believes that their resilience would be sufficient for them to rise again. But I do not think so.

He says Hindutva and Zionism are similar, and argues that India will not rise much as a nation-state, but instead rise as a diaspora.

I do agree with him to an extent here. But concluding that Indians would help in the greater Israel project is neither probable nor reasonable.

The Predictions That Came True

Jiang's reputation is partly built around predictions that appeared to come true. Two examples are frequently mentioned.

1. Donald Trump's 2024 Election Victory

Jiang forecast in May 2024 that Donald Trump would win the US presidential election later that year. Trump won in November 2024.

2. A US Conflict with Iran

Jiang predicted that Trump's administration would launch or escalate a war or direct conflict with Iran. Rising military tensions and subsequent hostilities have been presented as fulfilment of this forecast.

However, I do not think these predictions were particularly difficult to make. One could have said so.

Especially the victory of Trump. Regarding the Iran conflict, tensions were already appearing in the headlines. Predicting escalation was not especially difficult either.

And assuming that the US might lose a war of attrition is something that could happen. We have seen Afghanistan and Vietnam. But that does not mean the same outcome will occur every time.

The Third-Term Prediction

And regarding Trump's third term? I do not seriously suppose that this is practical without amending the constitutional provisions concerning presidential terms. He cannot simply run for a third term under the current rules.

I find the idea highly impractical, not only due to constitutional reasons, but also for other reasons.

Will the Republicans keep him as their leader? Would he remain the leader for more than a decade? That is a serious question.

Will Americans trust Trump again? Probably not, if the Democratic candidate is able to handle things well.

A third term is not practical without constitutional reform.

My Final Assessment

Jiang, famous as "Prof. Jiang Xueqin", initially made me hesitate to call him a professor. Not because I care about formality. I suspected that his framework was not one that should be trusted.

His "speciality" is not so "special". But his views are problematic. That is all.

He has surely read a few writers who could be called intellectuals. But betting on their frameworks alone is not something one can fully rely upon. He must be heavily doubted.

Before, I found a few of his views insufficient, but I still remained at the position of: "At least the man is honest."

Now, I find him problematic.

End of essay.
