The two sides of a Coin.

Today, I’m discussing something that we are told by someone who says that we are only looking at one side of a thing, not the other. They say that we are only looking at one aspect, not the other. We are only holding one perspective not the other.

The problem with this argument is that once we assume that there could be other views as well, in one of those perspectives there is also a perspective that says that there is only one perspective and it is theirs. But you then consider it a wrong and flawed argument or consider it a fallacy. But there is one more step now, you think that while one must consider other perspectives too, but a perspective in which there is only one right perspective, is wrong and is fallacy.

You are essentially saying something similar to what you are opposing, you are opposing that the perspective that assumes it is the only right one, is necessarily wrong. Yet you think that you perspective of considering multiple aspects and perspectives is closer to reality. Yet we can still say “Believing that the truth is often in diversified and open perspective” is neither an established fact nor can you say the superior perspective.

It is quite similar to saying “I prefer balance rather than binary thoughts. I prefer balance over bias”. But hey, to avoid bias, you have balance. But then you have bias to balance, you are extreme to balance. Does that view look like not extreme and unbiased to you? No, in fact, you are unrealisingly reaching a point where you assume that the disease itself is the cure.

Have you ever thought about it?
