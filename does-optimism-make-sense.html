Does Optimism make sense?

The previous thought was exactly the same in which we talked about Pessimism instead of Optimism and it did make sense. Today, we are looking towards the opposite of it, and we are going to figure out whether it makes sense or does it not?

What do you mean? Optimism means that you’re hopeful about so thing specific that is going to happen in the future, you’re assuming that it is all going to go well. Whether it is real or a construct, it still drives us to live a happy life but is it enough to declare that it becomes valid therefore.

Let’s say you’re hopeful about tomorrow’s test, but you eventually find it difficult to tackle, but you still remain hopeful to get out done without forgetting whatsoever you remember now. Let’s say you’ve made a life decision, you’re now ambitious about what will happen, you comfort yourself by giving yourself hope whether constructed or real or a blend.

Optimism becomes a great tool whenever we are in a situation where we find ourselves defeated, whether or not will the defeat would actually matter after a matter of time. Without optimism, one sees a problem in every opportunity. Yes, there are. But the illusion of optimism helps us to move ahead even when the path is unclear.

It may not be true, but it is a great tool. But blending it with reality, through adding it up with a real plan makes it worth. That doesn’t entirely depend on a single outcome, that isn’t mere construction. It should have a mix, at the end. Only then will it work. Can you imagine Napoleon winning if he only thought he’d lose? No. That’s why it is a great tool.
